About
"Every society has believed in something it cannot see. I want to know why."

I'm Ritul, a lawyer. I was brought up in Kolkata, but my family comes from Bhawanipore, a village in Bihar.

Bhawanipore has a story. The poet Vidyapati sang to Shiva with so much love that, people say, Shiva came down just to listen. He took the form of a servant called Ugna, so he could stay close to the songs. The moment his secret was spoken aloud, Ugna disappeared, and Vidyapati spent the rest of his life calling for him.

I think of that story every time a song stops me in my tracks. I'm only human, and a good song still mesmerises me. In those moments it feels as if something is listening too. Maybe that is where belief begins.

The Good God Question is my way of asking why. I look at where belief came from, what it does for people, and why it still shapes politics, love and war. I read the research in psychology, history, anthropology and philosophy, and explain it in plain language. And every episode ends with songs, because some of the best thinking about God has happened in music.

I'm not here to defend any faith, or to mock one. I want to understand it.
